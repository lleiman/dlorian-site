THE DEATH

Seven frames. One room.Visual sequence / work in progress
01   /   THE WORK

The distance from a death.

In 65 CE, Seneca was ordered to take his own life. Tacitus describes a death that refused to arrive quickly: opened veins, poison that failed, heated water, and finally the steam of a bath.

These seven frames hold the last hours inside a single invented chamber. The viewpoint moves between architecture, body, object and witness. Each change of scale alters what can be understood. The event remains the same; our distance from it does not.

The first image establishes the world: almost total darkness, a low red light, a central figure made small by the room around him. The last will return to precisely that space, once everyone has gone.

03   /   HISTORICAL RECORD

An account. An invention.

The historical point of departure is Tacitus, Annals, Book XV, chapters 60–64. The written account supplies the sequence of Seneca's final hours. The hall, its statue, the precise arrangement of bodies and the direction of light belong to this artwork.

The seven views follow an editorial rhythm rather than a literal minute-by-minute reconstruction. The return to an empty room is the final image, not an additional historical claim.
